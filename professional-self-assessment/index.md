# Professional Self-Assessment

## Introduction

Looking back at everything I have completed throughout my Computer Software Engineering program, I can see how much my understanding of software development has changed. When I first started the program, I was mostly focused on learning how to write code and getting my programs to work. As I moved through different courses, I started to understand that software engineering involves much more than programming. I have had to learn how to understand requirements, design systems, work with databases, think through problems, consider security, communicate ideas, and make decisions about how different parts of a system should work together.

Creating this ePortfolio has given me a chance to look back at that progression and see how the different parts of my coursework connect. Some of the projects I completed were difficult at the time, but looking at them now makes it easier to recognize the skills I developed from them. It has also helped me identify the areas of software engineering that I enjoy the most and the type of work I want to continue doing after graduation.

One of the biggest strengths I developed throughout the program is problem solving. There were many times when I did not immediately know how to solve a problem or why something in my code was not working. I had to research the problem, test different solutions, and figure out what worked. That experience taught me to be more comfortable with problems that I do not immediately know how to solve. I know that this is something I will continue to deal with as a software engineer because there will always be new technologies, unfamiliar code, and problems that require some trial and error.

## Collaborating in a Team Environment

Even though much of my coursework was completed individually, my projects helped me understand what it means to develop software as part of a larger team. I had to think about how different people would use a system, how different parts of an application would connect, and how someone else might need to understand or work with the code I created.

The DriverPass project in CS 255 was a good example of this. I had to think about the system from the perspective of different users instead of only thinking about what I personally wanted the application to do. The project involved requirements, system design, UML diagrams, and planning how the different parts of the system would work together. This helped me understand that software development is a collaborative process even when I am the person doing the actual work.

I also learned that being able to accept feedback and make changes is an important part of working on software. A project can change as new information becomes available, and being able to adjust the design or implementation is just as important as having the original idea.

## Communicating With Stakeholders

Another skill I developed throughout the program is communicating technical ideas in a way that makes sense to different people. Software engineers cannot always assume that the person asking for an application understands the technical side of how it will be built. I learned that I have to understand what someone actually needs before deciding how to build it.

The DriverPass project helped me with this because I had to consider the needs of the people who would actually use the system. The requirements were not just a list of things to program. They represented problems that the system needed to solve. This changed the way I think about requirements because I started paying more attention to the reason behind a feature instead of only thinking about how I would code it.

This is something I want to continue improving as I enter the software engineering field. I want to be able to communicate with both technical and nontechnical people and make sure that I understand what they are actually asking for before I start developing a solution.

## Data Structures and Algorithms

Data structures and algorithms have also played an important role in my development as a programmer. Throughout the program, I learned that getting a program to work is not always enough. The way information is organized and the way a problem is solved can affect how efficiently a program runs.

Working with different data structures and algorithms helped me become more thoughtful about how I approach programming problems. Instead of immediately trying to write code, I became more comfortable breaking a problem down into smaller parts and thinking about what type of solution makes the most sense.

This was especially helpful in assignments where I had to work with larger amounts of information or find ways to search, organize, and process data. These concepts are important because they are not limited to one programming language or one type of application. They are part of the foundation of software development and are skills I will continue to use regardless of the type of software I work on.

## Software Engineering and Databases

Software engineering has been one of the biggest parts of my degree because I have been able to see how the different parts of development fit together. Throughout my coursework, I worked with requirements, system design, implementation, testing, debugging, and maintenance. Courses such as CS 250, CS 255, and CS 305 helped me understand that software development is a process rather than simply writing code until a program works.

I also gained a lot of experience working with databases. In CS 340, I worked with Python and MongoDB while developing the Animal Shelter Dashboard. This gave me experience connecting an application to a database and working with stored information through CRUD operations. It also helped me understand how important it is for an application to be able to organize, retrieve, and update information efficiently.

My full-stack development coursework also helped me understand how databases fit into a larger application. Working with technologies such as MongoDB, Express, Angular, and Node allowed me to see how the front end, back end, and database communicate with each other. These experiences helped me become more comfortable thinking about an application as a complete system instead of focusing on one individual piece.

## Security

Security is another area that became much more important to me as I moved through the program. Before taking my cybersecurity and secure coding courses, I mostly thought about whether software worked correctly. I learned that software can work exactly as intended and still have serious security problems.

Through my secure coding coursework, I worked with issues such as unsafe input handling, hard-coded database credentials, insecure communication, and vulnerable dependencies. Finding these problems helped me understand how easily security issues can become part of an application if they are not considered during development.

This changed the way I think about writing software because security should not be something that is checked only at the end of a project. It should be considered while the software is being designed and developed. I want to carry that mindset with me into my career because protecting user information and building trustworthy applications is an important part of being a good software engineer.

## Professional Growth and Career Goals

Overall, completing this program has helped me become more confident in my ability to learn and work through problems. I would not say that I know everything about software engineering, but I have learned how to figure things out when I do not know the answer. That is probably one of the most important things I am taking away from this program.

My coursework also helped me figure out what I want to do professionally. I am interested in becoming a software engineer and developing applications that solve real problems. I especially like the idea of working on applications where the software can make something easier, more accessible, or more useful for the people using it. I am interested in continuing to build my skills in application development while gaining experience in a professional environment.

The ePortfolio has also helped me become more comfortable presenting my work. Instead of only saying that I have experience with programming, databases, software design, or algorithms, I can now show examples of the work I have completed. It gives me a place to explain what I worked on, what I learned, and how those experiences have contributed to my development.

I also think the process of creating this portfolio has helped me become more aware of my own progress. Some of the projects included here come from courses I completed earlier in the program, and looking back at them now makes it easier to see how my skills have changed. I can see areas where I would approach a problem differently today, which is a good reminder that becoming a better software engineer is an ongoing process.

## Portfolio and Artifact Overview

The artifacts included in this ePortfolio were chosen because they show different parts of my development throughout the program. I did not want the portfolio to focus on only one type of programming or one type of project. Instead, I wanted the artifacts to show a range of skills and give a better idea of the different areas of software engineering I have worked with.

The **Software Design and Engineering** section includes my CS 330 Computational Graphics and Visualization project. This project gave me experience working with C++, OpenGL, Visual Studio, 3D objects, textures, and scene development. It shows my ability to take a specific design and turn it into a working 3D software project.

The **Algorithms and Data Structures** section focuses on my experience with problem solving and developing solutions for working with data. This area represents the programming concepts I learned throughout the program and how I learned to think about the organization and efficiency of different solutions.

The **Databases** section includes my Animal Shelter Dashboard from CS 340. This project uses Python and MongoDB to store and manage animal records. It shows my experience working with a database from an application and performing operations such as creating, reading, updating, and deleting information.

The **Code Review** section focuses on my ability to look at existing software and identify areas that could be improved. Instead of only creating something from scratch, the code review gave me an opportunity to evaluate an existing project, identify problems, and think about how the software could be improved.

Together, these artifacts show different parts of what I have learned throughout the program. The projects involve different technologies and different types of problems, but they are connected by the same basic skills of problem solving, development, testing, and improvement. They also show that software engineering involves more than just writing code. It involves understanding what a system needs to do, deciding how it should work, managing information, thinking about security, and being able to evaluate and improve the final product.
